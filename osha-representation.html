OSHA Showed Up. Now What?

Calm, organized help for employers facing an OSHA inspection, a document request, or a citation, from the first knock to the final abatement date.

CompShield helps employers respond to OSHA the right way: prepared, organized, and on schedule. We work with construction, healthcare, staffing, and other employers with real exposure to workplace injuries, the same employers we defend on the workers' comp claims side every day. When OSHA opens an inspection or issues a citation, the quality of your response in the first days shapes everything that follows.

One thing worth knowing up front: an inspection does not necessarily mean a violation or citation has been issued. OSHA cites only where it determines a violation occurred, and some inspections close with no citation at all. Either way, what you say, what you hand over, and how fast you move becomes the record the decision is based on.

CompShield's team includes an OSHA specialist who handles citation and compliance matters, and our prevention work already covers safety and loss prevention guidance, root cause analysis, and corrective action tracking. This page explains how we put that to work during an inspection and after a citation.

If OSHA is on site now, or an inspection is open

During an active inspection, small mistakes compound: records that cannot be found, answers that wander beyond the question, corrective fixes made with no documentation. We help you run the inspection like a process instead of a scramble.

  • Inspection preparation: get your written programs, training records, and injury logs located and organized before the walkaround, and know what the opening conference will cover
  • Records, organized: pull the OSHA 300 logs, 300A summaries, and 301 reports the officer will ask for. Injury and illness records must be produced within four business hours of a request, so this is not a someday task
  • A coordinated response: one point of contact, questions routed consistently, and a written log of what was requested, what was provided, and what was said
  • Closing conference readiness: understand what the officer found, what happens next, and what to fix now versus what to evaluate once you see it in writing

If you have received a citation

A citation starts a clock. Under federal OSHA you have 15 working days from receipt to contest the citation, the proposed penalty, and/or the abatement dates in writing. Miss that window and the citation becomes a final order that no court or agency can review. We help you use the window well:

  • Citation review: read each item against the standard cited, the evidence behind it, and your actual workplace conditions
  • Informal conference strategy: prepare for the meeting with the Area Director, where penalties, classifications, and abatement dates can be discussed. The conference does not pause the 15 working day clock, so it has to be requested early and run in parallel
  • Contest decision support: evaluate what to contest, what to settle, and what to simply correct, with the tradeoffs in plain language
  • Abatement planning: build a realistic correction plan for each item, and, where a deadline cannot be met despite a good faith effort, prepare a Petition for Modification of Abatement before the date passes
  • The claims connection: an OSHA citation and a workers' comp claim often grow out of the same incident. We manage both sides, so your citation response and your claim defense do not work against each other

How it works

  • 1. Initial discussion. A short call about where things stand: inspection open, citation in hand, or a document request you are not sure how to answer. No obligation, and no documents needed for the first conversation
  • 2. Review of the situation and documents. We go through the citation or inspection details, your records, and the deadlines in play, and tell you plainly what we see, including if the right move is simply to correct and pay, or to bring in your attorney

Frequently asked questions

OSHA is at my door. What happens during an inspection?

An inspection starts with an opening conference. The compliance officer shows credentials, explains why OSHA is there and what the inspection will cover, and goes over your rights. Then comes a walkaround of the workplace: an employer representative and an employee representative have the right to accompany the officer. It ends with a closing conference where the officer discusses what was found. No citation is issued at the closing conference. The officer reports back to the OSHA Area Director, who decides whether a citation is warranted.

Does an OSHA inspection mean I am getting a citation?

No. An inspection does not necessarily mean a violation or citation has been issued. OSHA issues a citation only if it determines that a violation occurred. Some inspections close with no citation at all. What happens during the inspection still matters, because the officer's notes, photos, and interviews become the record any later decision is based on.

What records can OSHA ask for, and how fast do I have to produce them?

Expect requests for your OSHA 300 logs, 300A summaries, and 301 incident reports for the prior three years plus the current year, along with written programs such as hazard communication, lockout/tagout, emergency evacuation, and PPE. Under federal rules, injury and illness records kept under Part 1904 must be provided within four business hours of a request by an authorized government representative. Knowing where these records are before OSHA asks is half the battle.

I received a citation. How long do I have to respond?

Under federal OSHA, you have 15 working days from the day you receive the citation to file a written notice of contest covering the citation, the proposed penalty, and/or the abatement date. Working days are Monday through Friday, excluding federal holidays, and the notice must be postmarked by the 15th working day. If you miss the window, the citation becomes a final order that no court or agency can review. This deadline is the single most important date in the process.

What is an informal conference, and should I request one?

An informal conference is a meeting with the OSHA Area Director to discuss the citation: the violations cited, the standards that apply, how to correct them, the proposed penalties, and the abatement dates. It can lead to an informal settlement or an amended citation. One warning: requesting or holding an informal conference does not pause or extend the 15 working day contest deadline. Request it early, and if the conference does not resolve the matter, file your written notice of contest within the 15 working days anyway.

What is an abatement date? What if we cannot finish the fix in time?

The abatement date is the deadline in the citation by which the cited condition must be corrected. If you agree the condition needs fixing but need more time, raise it at the informal conference: the Area Director can amend the citation with a later date. After the contest period has passed, an extension requires a written Petition for Modification of Abatement, filed with the Area Director no later than the close of the next working day after the original abatement date, showing a good faith effort and factors beyond your reasonable control.

When does a citation actually arrive after an inspection?

Citations are usually mailed after the closing conference, but OSHA has up to six months from the occurrence of a violation to issue one. Silence in the first few weeks does not mean the inspection closed without action. Use that time to get your records and your response ready.

Do the same rules apply in New York and New Jersey?

For private employers, yes. The New York and New Jersey state plans (PESH and PEOSH) cover public sector workers only, so private employers in both states are under federal OSHA and the federal deadlines above apply. In states that run their own OSHA plan for private employers, such as Utah or California, the citation itself carries that plan's deadlines, which can differ. Check the paperwork you received.

How much are OSHA penalties?

Proposed penalties are printed in the citation and depend on the type and seriousness of each violation, adjusted for factors like employer size, good faith, and history. Federal maximum penalties are published at osha.gov/penalties and change periodically. Whether to contest a penalty is a case by case decision, which is part of what the initial review covers.

When should I get help?

As early as possible. During an active inspection, help with preparation and records can shape the record the decision is based on. After a citation, the 15 working day contest window starts on receipt, not when you get around to opening the envelope. The practical rule: if OSHA has visited, or a citation has arrived, make the call that week.
