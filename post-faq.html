Workers' Comp Questions, Answered Straight

What CompShield does, what the Audit covers and costs, who it is for, and how to start. No sales talk, just the facts employers and brokers ask about most.

What does CompShield do?

CompShield is a workers' compensation consulting firm for employers. It helps employers defend claims, investigate fraud, contain costs, and get injured workers back to work. The work covers four areas: safety before an accident, investigation when one happens, carrier oversight and reserves after a claim is filed, and loss run and experience mod review.

What is the CompShield Audit?

The CompShield Audit is a flat-fee review of an employer's workers' compensation program across four pillars: safety before the accident, investigation when it happens, carrier oversight and reserves after filing, and loss run and mod review. The deliverable is a Findings Report. The fee is $3,500 for employers under about 150 employees and $5,500 for employers with 150 to 500 employees. The audit fee is credited toward an advisory retainer if the employer continues with CompShield. CompShield does not guarantee savings and does not charge contingency pricing.

Who is CompShield for?

Employers in New York, especially in the New York City boroughs, with about 75 to 250 employees spending $50,000 to $500,000 a year on workers' compensation. CompShield specializes in healthcare and construction, and usually works with owners, CFOs, and HR directors.

What is a Code Rule 59 consultation, and can CompShield perform one?

New York Code Rule 59 requires certain employers with high experience mods to hire a certified workplace safety and loss prevention consultant. CompShield's consultant is certified by the New York Department of Labor for Code Rule 59 and Code Rule 60 consultations (certification WS-000795, active through March 31, 2028) and can perform the mandated consultation. Employers who received a Code Rule 59 notice can start with a forensic audit at $2,500, credited toward ongoing claims management.

How does an employer start?

With a free 15-minute workers' comp cost review. CompShield looks at the employer's open claims, reserves, and mod, and shows where the money is going. Contact runs through the form at comp-shield.com, which delivers to info@comp-shield.com.

Does CompShield work with insurance brokers?

Yes. Brokers refer employer clients for audits. A broker receives $500 for each completed audit that came from their referral, paid after the audit is delivered, plus 10 percent of the first year if the client becomes an ongoing engagement.
