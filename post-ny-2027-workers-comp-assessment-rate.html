New York Holds the 2027 Workers' Comp Assessment Rate Flat at 7%

On October 20, 2026, the New York State Workers' Compensation Board announced that the assessment rate for calendar year 2027 will be 7% of standard premium. That is the same rate as 2026. There is no increase to worry about, but there is also no extra cut coming. After several years of steady declines, this part of your comp bill stays exactly where it is.

This article summarizes a Workers' Compensation Board announcement in general terms and is not legal or insurance advice.

The announcement is short. In Subject Number 046-1838, dated October 20, 2026, Board Chair Freida D. Foster set the rate under Workers' Compensation Law Section 151. The Chair sets this rate every year by November 1, and it takes effect on January 1 of the next year. For 2027, the rate is 7% of the standard premium or premium equivalent.

What the assessment is

The assessment is a mandatory surcharge added to workers' comp policies in New York. It funds the Workers' Compensation Board and related programs, including the special funds. Carriers apply the rate to your standard premium and pass it through on your bill. If you are self-insured, the same rate applies to your premium equivalent instead. It is not optional, and it is not set by your carrier. Every employer in the state pays the same percentage.

The math is simple. On a policy with $100,000 of standard premium, a 7% assessment adds $7,000. On $250,000 of standard premium, it adds $17,500. When you compare quotes or build a budget, that line is part of the real total, not a footnote.

The run of cuts has stopped

For years, this rate moved in one direction. It was 12.6% in 2019. It fell to 9.2% for 2024, then dropped sharply to 7.1% for 2025, and eased to 7.0% for 2026. A lot of that decline came from the state working down old special fund liabilities that the assessment pays for.

For 2027, the rate does not move at all. It holds at 7.0%. That is still far below where it stood a few years ago, and a flat rate is better news than an increase. But if your 2027 budget assumed another assessment cut on top of everything else, take that assumption out. There is no second round of relief hiding in this line.

How this fits with the October 1 loss cost cut

The bigger rate news this fall was the 21.9% average loss cost decrease that took effect on October 1, 2026, for policies renewing on or after that date. The assessment interacts with that cut in a way worth understanding. The assessment is a percentage of standard premium, so if your standard premium goes down at renewal, the dollar amount of your assessment goes down with it, even though the percentage is unchanged. If your premium goes up because of your class codes, your payroll, or your experience mod, your assessment dollars go up too. The percentage is fixed. The base it rides on is yours.

That is the practical point. Albany controls the 7%. You and your broker control most of what sits underneath it: correct class codes, accurate payroll, and a mod that reflects claims that were handled well and closed properly.

What to do now

  • Budget 7% for 2027. If your policy renews on or after January 1, 2027, build the assessment in at 7% of standard premium. No increase, no decrease.
  • Check the base, not just the rate. When a quote arrives, confirm the assessment is being charged on standard premium and look at what is driving that standard premium. Errors in class codes or payroll flow straight into the assessment too.
  • Do not double count relief. The October loss cost cut and the assessment are separate lines. A lower premium helps both lines. The assessment rate itself gives you nothing new in 2027.
  • Self-insured employers, same rate. The 7% applies to premium equivalent, so the flat year applies to you in the same way.

The part of the bill you can still move

A flat assessment rate means one less thing changes in 2027, and one less excuse for a sloppy renewal. The state has done its part on rates for now. What is left is the part employers can actually check: whether the payroll, class codes, loss runs, and mod behind the premium are right. Small errors there cost more than a tenth of a point on an assessment ever will.
